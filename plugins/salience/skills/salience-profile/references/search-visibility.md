# Recruiter and Executive-Search Visibility

Being found is a different problem from being persuasive, and it is solved with different moves.
A profile can be excellent and invisible.

Important distinction at this level: **most CMO and VP roles are not filled through LinkedIn
Recruiter keyword searches.** They are filled through retained search firms and referral. Optimize
for search visibility, but do not mistake it for the primary channel — the relationship work in
`salience-engage` and `salience-career` carries more weight for senior roles. Say so plainly rather
than implying keyword tuning will produce a CMO seat.

## How the surface works

Recruiters and search associates run keyword queries against a title-and-skill vocabulary, then
filter by geography, seniority, industry, and current company. To appear:

1. **The role noun must be present in the words they type.** They search "CMO", "VP Marketing",
   "Head of Marketing" — not "growth architect" or "marketing visionary". Creative titles are
   invisible to search. Keep the creative claim, but carry the standard noun somewhere indexed.
2. **Headline and Skills carry the most weight**, then Experience titles, then About.
3. **Skills need endorsements to count.** An unendorsed skill contributes little.
4. **Recency matters.** Dormant profiles are deprioritized. Activity is itself a ranking input.
5. **Completeness matters.** Incomplete profiles rank lower regardless of quality.

## Vocabulary audit

Build the target vocabulary from real postings, not from imagination:

1. Collect 8–12 job descriptions for the target role and company profile.
2. Extract the recurring nouns — titles, functions, technologies, methodologies.
3. Score presence on the profile: headline, skills, experience titles, About.
4. Report what is missing that the user can honestly claim.

Only add terms the user can defend. A keyword that wins a search and loses the screening call is
worse than absence — it costs credibility with the exact person who matters.

## Settings that change discovery

Walk through these; several are frequently missed:

- **Open to Work, recruiters-only** — reaches LinkedIn Recruiter users without the public green
  badge. For a sitting executive this is usually the right setting; the public badge signals
  availability to clients, competitors, and the current employer.
- **Target role preferences** — titles, locations, start timing, employment types (include
  contract/fractional if advisory work is in scope)
- **Public profile visibility** — full public profile on
- **Custom URL** — claimed
- **Industry and location** — set accurately; both are hard filters in search
- **"Providing services"** — turn on for consulting and fractional work; it creates a separate
  discovery surface most executives never enable
- **Creator mode / follow-first** — appropriate for authority goals; changes the primary button from
  Connect to Follow, which suits someone building an audience and hurts someone building a network

## Geography

Location is a hard filter. An executive open to relocation or remote work who lists only a metro
area is filtered out of searches they would take. Set the location honestly and use About and role
preferences to state openness to remote or relocation explicitly.

## What not to do

- **Never keyword-stuff.** "CMO | Chief Marketing Officer | Marketing Executive | Marketing Leader |
  Head of Marketing" reads as desperation to the human who opens the profile, and the profile is
  read by a human immediately after the search returns it.
- **Never list a skill the user cannot discuss under questioning.**
- **Never fabricate a title.** Adjusting a title to its industry-standard equivalent is reasonable
  when the internal title was idiosyncratic ("Marketing Wizard" → "Head of Marketing"); inventing a
  seniority level is not, and it fails the first reference check.

## Measurement

Track over 30 days after changes: search appearances, profile views, viewer composition (recruiters,
peers, target companies), and inbound message quality.

Viewer composition is the signal that matters. Rising views from unrelated industries means the
keywords are wrong, not working. Hand this to `salience-analytics`.
