# Photo and Banner

The first two seconds. A visitor forms a judgment before reading a word, and at executive level the
judgment being formed is about credibility, not attractiveness.

## Photo

### Specification
- Minimum 400×400 px, square. Upload larger; LinkedIn downsamples well and upsamples badly
- Face fills roughly 60% of the frame — head and shoulders, not full body
- Direct eye contact with the camera
- Natural light where possible; a window beats most ring lights
- Uncluttered background, solid or softly blurred
- Attire matching the room being targeted, one notch more formal than daily wear
- Slight smile. Warmth reads as confidence; a hard neutral expression reads as defensive

### Currency
Update every 2–3 years. A photo visibly older than the person creates a small credibility problem at
the exact moment it matters — the first in-person meeting or the video call.

### Kills credibility
Cropped group photo with someone else's shoulder · sunglasses or hat · vacation setting · visible
heavy retouching · low resolution · facing away · an obvious stock headshot · a logo instead of a
face on a personal profile

### Executive note
A professionally shot photo is worth the modest cost at this level, and it is distinguishable from a
phone photo in a way the audience notices without being able to articulate. It is the cheapest
credibility purchase on this list.

## Banner

1584×396 px (4:1).

### Composition

**Keep everything meaningful in the right two-thirds.** The profile photo covers the lower left on
desktop and overlaps further on mobile. Text placed left is lost.

Include:
- The value proposition, in a few words — usually a compression of the headline claim
- High contrast: a solid field or a single brand color beats a photograph
- One CTA if relevant — a URL, or "Advisory & fractional engagements"
- A small logo or mark, cornered

Limit to three colors. Type large enough to read at phone width.

### Mobile crop test
Before finalizing, check at phone portrait, tablet, and desktop. LinkedIn crops aggressively and
mobile is where most of the traffic is. If the value proposition is unreadable on a phone, the
banner does not work, however good it looks on a laptop.

### Goal-matched content

- **Executive search** — positioning statement and the domains of expertise. No "Open to Work"
  overlay on an executive profile; it reads as availability rather than selectivity. Use the
  recruiter-visible setting instead, which reaches search professionals without broadcasting.
- **Consulting** — the offer and a path. "Fractional CMO and marketing advisory for B2B SaaS ·
  morganreyes.example"
- **Authority** — the point of view or the owned channel.

### Anti-patterns
LinkedIn's default blue · stock handshake · stock skyline · a motivational quote · text sitting
behind the profile photo · more than three colors · a busy photograph that fights the type · a
company banner on a personal profile the user does not own

### Why it matters
The banner is the largest uncontested surface on the profile and the most consistently wasted. For a
consultant it is prime advertising space directly above the fold; leaving it on the default is the
equivalent of an unbranded storefront.
