# Language Scan

Run before scoring. Every flag gets a **specific replacement**, not a note — "avoid buzzwords" is
advice nobody can act on.

The test for every flagged phrase: *could a materially different person write this exact sentence
about themselves?* If yes, it is not describing anyone.

## Empty-signal claims

| Flagged | Why | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| results-driven / results-oriented | Describes no result | The result |
| passionate about | Unfalsifiable | What they did about it |
| dynamic professional | Says nothing | The specific capability |
| proven track record | Assertion without the record | The record |
| seasoned professional | Age signal, no content | Scope and outcome |
| detail-oriented | Expected, not distinguishing | An outcome detail produced |
| team player | Expected of everyone | A specific collaboration outcome |
| go-getter | Reads junior | Something they went and got |
| strategic thinker | Everyone claims it | The strategy and its result |
| visionary | Self-applied and unverifiable | The call made and how it turned out |
| thought leader (self-applied) | Only credible from others | The body of work |
| hard worker | Baseline | Scope carried |

## Executive-specific

These four appear on nearly every VP-and-above profile, which is exactly why they no longer signal
anything:

| Flagged | Replace with |
|---|---|
| transformational leader | The transformation: from what, to what, over how long |
| change agent | The change and its measured effect |
| builder of high-performing teams | Team size, what it shipped, where people went next |
| data-driven decision maker | A decision the data drove and what it produced |

Also flag: *servant leader* · *player-coach* (unless genuinely describing scope) · *trusted advisor*
· *C-suite executive* (the title already says this) · *20+ years of experience* — at executive level
this adds no information and can invite age filtering. Replace with the scope that experience
produced.

## AI-writing tells

Profiles rewritten by an assistant acquire a recognizable register. To this audience it reads as
outsourced, which undercuts the authority the copy is claiming.

**Vocabulary:** leverage (as verb) · delve · unlock · harness · foster · streamline · robust ·
comprehensive · fundamentally · seamless · cutting-edge · game-changing · revolutionary · elevate ·
empower · navigate (metaphorical) · landscape (metaphorical) · realm · tapestry · testament to

**Constructions:**
- "It's not just X, it's Y"
- "In today's fast-paced landscape…"
- "I'm excited to announce/share…"
- Rule-of-three lists everywhere ("strategy, execution, and results")
- Em-dash-heavy rhythm in copy the person would not otherwise write that way
- Every paragraph the same length
- Closing with a summary of what was just said

**Punctuation:** emoji bullets in an executive About · arrow bullets (→) used decoratively rather
than structurally · ALL CAPS for emphasis · exclamation points

## Hedging

Weak copy is not the same as honest copy. Hedging inside published copy is the wrong place to
express uncertainty — the caveat belongs in `[VERIFY]`, and the line itself should be strong and
true.

| Hedged | Direct |
|---|---|
| helped to improve | improved |
| was involved in growing | grew (or: led the team that grew) |
| played a key role in | led / built / shipped |
| contributed to a 40% increase | drove a 40% increase — *or*, if accurate, "one of three leads on the initiative that drove 40%" |
| responsible for driving | drove |

The correct fix for an overstated claim is a **more precise** claim, not a vaguer one.

## Reporting

```
Language scan — 7 flags

Headline
  "Results-driven marketing executive"
  → "Marketing executive for B2B SaaS — 34% lower CAC at Hollis"
     "Results-driven" describes no result; you have a verified one.

About, line 1
  "Passionate about building high-performing teams"
  → "Built a 22-person marketing org across 3 business units; 3 people promoted to director"
     Same claim, now checkable.

About, line 6
  "Leveraging cutting-edge AI to transform the marketing landscape"
  → "I use AI where it removes work, not where it makes a deck look modern"
     Three AI-writing tells in one sentence; the replacement also says something.
```

Show the flag, the replacement, and one line of reasoning. The reasoning is what stops the same
phrase reappearing in the next draft.
