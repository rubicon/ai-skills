# Featured

The only section where the user chooses what a visitor sees first. Roughly four in five profiles
leave it empty or fill it with defaults, which makes it one of the few genuinely free advantages
still available on the platform.

For a consultant or advisor it is the conversion surface. For an executive in search it is
supporting evidence. Weight accordingly.

## Rules

- **Three strong items beats ten weak ones.** Featured renders as a carousel; items past the third
  are rarely seen.
- **Custom thumbnails, 1200×627**, with benefit-driven titles rather than descriptive ones.
  - Not "My blog post on marketing ops" → "How we cut a 14-tool MarTech stack down to 6"
  - Not "Case study PDF" → "B2B SaaS pipeline teardown, with the real numbers"
- **Rotate quarterly.** Anything older than about a year signals a dormant profile.
- **Everything featured must be something the user is willing to be asked about in detail.**
- **Weak content is worse than an empty section.** A three-year-old post with four reactions
  actively signals dormancy; nothing signals only absence. If the user has nothing strong, say so
  and treat "create one thing worth featuring" as the recommendation rather than filling slots.

## Goal-matched selection

### Executive search
1. **A signature piece of work** — a talk, a published framework, a case study with real outcomes
2. **Evidence of scope** — a board-level deck (sanitized), a transformation write-up, a team-building piece
3. **A strong point of view** — the article or post that best shows how the user thinks

Search partners use Featured to confirm the person is real and current. It is corroboration, not
persuasion.

### Consulting and advisory
1. **A lead asset that solves one acute problem** — an audit checklist, a diagnostic, a teardown template
2. **A named result** — client (or anonymized profile), the situation, the specific outcome
3. **A direct booking path** — a calendar link

This is a funnel. Diagnostic → proof → path. Ordering matters: proof before the ask.

### Authority
1. **The best thing the user has written**
2. **External validation** — a podcast appearance, a media mention, a conference talk
3. **The owned channel** — newsletter or subscription, moving the reader off LinkedIn

### Mixed
Lead with the primary goal's first item, take the second goal's strongest proof, close with the
primary goal's CTA. Do not alternate — a Featured row that alternates between "hire me" and "retain
me" reads as indecision to both audiences.

## Anti-patterns

| Pattern | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Empty | Forfeits the single best free advantage on the profile |
| The employer's corporate blog post | Features the company, not the person |
| A generic services PDF | Nobody downloads it |
| A 2019 webinar | Signals dormancy |
| Nine items | Nobody scrolls past three |
| Default carried over from a connection's post | Reads as an unattended profile |
| Screenshots with confidential client data | A judgment problem visible to every future client |

## Sanitization

Featured items frequently contain real client work. Before featuring anything:

- Confirm the user has the right to publish it
- Anonymize client names where the engagement was not public
- Remove revenue figures the client has not disclosed publicly
- Strip internal identifiers from screenshots

When in doubt, feature the anonymized version and record a gap. Featuring something a former client
objects to is a professional problem no amount of profile optimization repairs.
