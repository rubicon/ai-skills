# About

The only place on the profile with room to make an argument. Most executives waste it on a résumé
summary written in third person.

## Budgets

| Constraint | Value |
|---|---|
| Hard limit | 2,600 characters |
| Target | 200–300 words (roughly 1,200–1,800 characters) |
| Mobile cutoff | **First 265–275 characters** show before "…see more" |
| Voice | First person, always |

The hook is not a stylistic preference. Most readers never expand the section, so the first ~265
characters have to carry the entire pitch on their own. Draft the hook first, count it, and confirm
it survives the cut before writing anything else.

Do not pad to 2,600 characters. A tight 220-word About outperforms a complete one, and length past
about 300 words earns nothing on a section that is largely read above the fold.

## Seven-step structure

| Step | Job | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | A claim, question, or number that stops the right reader | ~50–120 chars |
| 2. What you do | The actual function and who it serves | ~100 chars |
| 3. Proof | One or two concrete outcomes with real numbers | ~150–250 chars |
| 4. How you do it | The method or point of view that makes the proof repeatable | ~200 chars |
| 5. Human line | One sentence that reads like a person wrote it | ~80 chars |
| 6. Specialties | Search keywords, written as a line rather than a list of buzzwords | ~150 chars |
| 7. CTA | One specific, low-friction next step | ~100 chars |

Steps 1–3 must fit inside the mobile cutoff wherever possible. That is the constraint that makes
this structure work.

## Worked example — executive search

```
Marketing works until the playbook stops compounding. Then it needs rebuilding, not more budget.

I run marketing for B2B SaaS companies at the point where growth flattens — brand, demand
generation, and the MarTech stack under one P&L.

At Hollis I rebuilt the acquisition engine around signal instead of volume: blended CAC down 34%
in 11 months, pipeline up 3.4x, with a team of 22 across three business units.

How I work: I start with the data layer, because most stalled funnels are a measurement problem
wearing a creative problem's clothes. Then brand and demand get rebuilt against what the data
actually says, not what the last agency deck promised.

Outside work: two kids, a stubborn espresso machine, and a long-running argument about attribution.

Specialties: marketing leadership, demand generation, marketing technology and automation,
AI-enabled marketing, performance marketing, brand strategy, B2B and B2C growth, distributed teams.

If you are hiring a CMO or weighing whether your growth problem is strategy or plumbing, message me
here — happy to give you a straight answer either way.
```

The first 265 characters land at "…brand, demand generation, and the MarTech stack under one P&L."
The reader knows who this is for and what the point of view is before deciding to expand.

Note what it does: opens on a claim rather than a name, quantifies once with a verified number,
names a real method, includes a genuinely human line, packs the specialties line with searchable
terms without listing adjectives, and closes on an offer that costs the reader nothing.

## Fork by goal

- **Executive search** — proof is scope and outcome. CTA invites a conversation with recruiters and
  hiring executives. Name target role words explicitly somewhere in the section.
- **Consulting and advisory** — proof is client results and repeatability. CTA points to a specific
  low-friction asset in Featured (an audit, a teardown, a calendar link).
- **Authority** — proof is point of view and body of work. CTA points to the owned channel:
  newsletter, podcast, or writing.
- **Mixed** — lead with the primary goal and resolve the secondary in one explicit sentence. Do not
  average the two; an About section that hedges between "hire me" and "retain me" does neither.

## Anti-patterns

| Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Third person ("Morgan is a seasoned...") | First person. Third person is a press release and suppresses replies |
| "Welcome to my profile!" / "Hi, I'm..." | Open on the claim. The name is displayed directly above |
| "Passionate about driving results" | Replace with the specific result |
| Wall of text | Line breaks every 2–3 sentences; mobile renders unbroken text as a grey slab |
| Best metric in paragraph four | Move it above the fold or lose it |
| "Let's connect!" | A dead CTA. Ask for something specific |
| Listing every skill ever used | The Specialties line carries keywords; the body carries argument |
| Hedged copy ("helped to potentially improve") | Weak is not the same as honest. Write the strong true line and flag the tier in `[VERIFY]` |

## Evidence rule

Every number in this section must trace to the fact ledger. If the strongest available proof is
qualitative, write it qualitatively and record the gap — an About section with one real number and
three honest sentences outperforms one with four invented numbers, and it survives the reference
call that the second one fails.
