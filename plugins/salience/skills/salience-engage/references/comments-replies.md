# Comments and Replies

The highest-leverage, lowest-cost move on the platform: someone else's audience, a fraction of the
effort a post takes.

## What earns an author reply

The author reply is the strongest signal LinkedIn produces — a specific person engaged directly,
and the thread is now visible to their whole audience. Comments that earn it:

| Pattern | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Adds a data point the post lacks | Gives the author something to respond to |
| Names a specific exception | Invites refinement rather than agreement |
| Extends the argument one step further | The author wants to see where it goes |
| Answers the closing question concretely | Most people answer vaguely or not at all |
| Respectful, specific disagreement | The most memorable comment type, and the rarest |

## What dies

- Restating the post's thesis in different words. The most common comment and the most transparent
- "Great post!" with elaboration that adds nothing
- A pitch, however soft
- A comment clearly written without reading the post
- Tagging people to manufacture reach
- Anything over ~400 characters, which reads as a hijack

## Length

200-350 characters is the working range. One idea, said well.

Longer is occasionally right — a substantive disagreement needs room — but a comment longer than the
post it answers reads as a monologue and the author usually will not engage.

## Structure

```
[The specific thing]   The data point, exception, or extension. Lead with it.
[Why it matters]       One line of consequence.
[The opening]          A question or an implicit invitation. Optional but usually right.
```

## Worked examples

**On a post arguing that brand budgets should be cut in a downturn:**

> Held brand flat through 2023 and cut demand instead. Demand recovered in two quarters; the
> competitors who cut brand are still paying more per lead than they were in 2022. The asymmetry
> isn't obvious until you're on the other side of it.

Adds a specific counterexample with a mechanism. Does not say "great post" and does not restate.

**Disagreeing well:**

> I think this holds for PLG and breaks for enterprise. In a nine-month cycle with six stakeholders,
> the "just talk to users" advice runs into the problem that the user and the buyer are different
> people with opposite incentives. Curious whether you've seen it work in that shape.

Names the boundary condition, explains why, and asks a real question. Disagreement without
aggression is memorable.

## Rules

- **Read the post first.** Salience drafts from actual text, never from a headline. If the content
  is unavailable, ask for it
- **Never name the user's own product** in a comment on someone else's post. Describe what they do
  only when directly relevant
- **Never comment to be seen.** Frequency without substance is visible and cheapens the profile
- **Approval before publishing**, every time

## Replies

- Respond while the thread is live. Momentum decays fast
- Shorter than the comment being answered
- Move to DM only when there is something specific to say. "Would love to connect" is not that
- Let dead threads die. Not every exchange needs a last word
- When an author replies, that person is now warm. Record it in the relationship file
