# Role Alignment

## 1. Extract requirements

Executive postings routinely list twelve requirements where three are real. Separate them and say
which is which.

**Signals a requirement is real:** it appears in the opening paragraph · **it recurs three or more
times**, often in different words · it is unusually specific · it names a situation rather than a
skill ("has taken a company through a re-platform") · it maps to something visibly broken at the
company.

**Signals it is boilerplate:** generic soft skills · a laundry list of tools · "10+ years of
experience" · anything appearing verbatim in every posting for the title · anything mentioned once
in a bulleted list and never again.

The repetition count is the most reliable single tell. A requirement someone cared about gets
restated; boilerplate is written once and forgotten.

### Dealbreakers

| Genuinely disqualifying | Not disqualifying |
|---|---|
| A license, clearance, or credential the user does not hold and cannot obtain | Years of experience modestly below the stated number |
| A location requirement the user will not meet | A "preferred" degree |
| Scope more than roughly double anything they have run | A learnable tool or platform |
| A named industry requirement with regulatory weight | Industry adjacency where the motion transfers |

Say which kind a gap is. Treating a learnable tool as a dealbreaker costs the user a real
opportunity; treating a missing clearance as learnable wastes weeks.

## 2. Score against the ledger

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Verified evidence. Cite the specific fact |
| **Partial** | Adjacent experience. Say exactly how it is adjacent |
| **Gap** | Nothing in the record |

Never inflate partial to strong. The screening call resolves it in ten minutes, and having claimed
it costs more than the gap did.

## 3. Weight by what the role actually needs

Missing one of three real requirements matters more than missing six of nine boilerplate ones. Score
on the real ones and say so.

```
Match: strong on 6 of 8 real requirements

Strong
  Scaled B2B SaaS marketing $20M->$200M          Hollis, verified
  MarTech consolidation and ownership             14->6 tools, verified
  Distributed team leadership                     22 across 3 units, verified
  Attribution and measurement rebuild             verified
  P&L ownership                                   verified
  Board-level communication                       verified

Partial
  PE-backed operating experience    Board reporting yes; PE sponsor no. The posting says
                                    "sponsor-backed environment" twice, so this is real.
  Category creation                 You repositioned an existing category. Adjacent, and
                                    the distinction matters to a founder who cares about it.

Gap
  International expansion (EMEA)    Nothing in the record.

Read: EMEA is the only true gap and it appears in the opening paragraph, which means it is
a requirement, not a wish. Worth addressing directly rather than hoping it goes unnoticed.
PE experience is the softer one - "sponsor-backed environment" is about operating rhythm,
and board reporting at Hollis is close enough to speak to credibly.
```

## 4. Strategy

- **What to lead with** — the two or three strongest matches, in the language of the posting
- **How to handle each gap** — address the real ones directly. An executive who names a gap and
  explains their plan reads as senior; one who hopes it goes unmentioned reads as evasive
- **Whether to apply at all**

**Recommend against applying when the fit is poor.** A tool that encourages every application wastes
the user's most limited resource and, with retained search firms, their credibility. Repeated
misfires with a firm are remembered.

```
Recommendation: apply, and lead with the measurement rebuild.

Address EMEA in the first conversation rather than the application. The honest version -
"I haven't run EMEA; I've run three business units with different buying motions, and the
transferable part is the operating model, not the geography" - is credible. Pretending it
isn't a gap is not.
```

## Overqualification is a real rejection

The failure mode nobody warns executives about. A candidate who exceeds the role on every dimension
reads as a flight risk, a threat to the hiring manager, or someone who will be bored in nine
months — and search firms screen for it explicitly because a placement that leaves inside a year
costs them the fee and the relationship.

Flag it when it applies, and say what would have to be true:

```
This is a strong match in the sense that will work against you. You exceed the stated
scope on team, budget, and revenue. Expect the question "why would you want this?"
in the first conversation, and expect it to be the real screen.

Worth applying only if you can answer it truthfully - the stage, the mandate, the
ownership, or the people. If the honest answer is "I need a job", they will hear it.
```

Never encourage a stretch downward without naming this. It is the most common way a strong
executive candidate is quietly passed over, and the user usually never learns why.

## Reading the posting itself

The posting is evidence about the company, not just about the role.

**Workload signals** — "wear many hats" · "fast-paced" · "hit the ground running" · "thrives in
ambiguity" · "self-starter". One is nothing. Three or more in an executive posting usually means
under-resourced, and the mandate will exceed the support.

**Culture signals** — "work hard, play hard" · "like a family" · "rockstar" · unlimited vacation
offered as a headline benefit.

**Compensation signals** — "competitive salary" with no range, where a range is customary or legally
required · equity-heavy with light cash · a range so wide it means the level is undecided.

**Executive-specific, and more informative than any of the above:**

- **No stated mandate.** A posting that lists responsibilities but never says what needs to change
  usually means the company has not agreed on it either. That argument will happen after the hire.
- **Reporting line unstated or hedged.** "Partners closely with the CEO" is not "reports to the CEO".
- **The seat's history is absent.** Why is it open? A newly created role, a departure, and a
  termination are three different jobs.
- **A reposted or long-open listing.** The first search failed, the role changed, or a hire did not
  work out. All three are worth knowing and none are automatically disqualifying — ask directly.
- **Marketing reporting into Sales**, or an equivalent structural subordination, where the posting
  claims strategic ownership.
- **A vague posting is itself a signal** at this level. Executive briefs are usually written
  carefully because the audience is small.

Report these as observations with an interpretation, never as verdicts. A posting with three
workload signals may still be the right job — the point is that the user goes into the first
conversation knowing what to probe.

## Before applying

Worth ten minutes, and it changes the decision more often than the match score does:

- Recent funding, layoffs, or leadership departures
- How long people in adjacent senior roles have stayed
- Whether the predecessor is findable and what they did next
- Whether the compensation range is consistent with comparable roles
- Patterns in the critical reviews, not the star rating

## Scope, not just skills

The most common executive mismatch is not capability, it is **scope**. A VP running an $8M budget
applying for a CMO seat with a $60M budget faces a real question regardless of skill overlap.

Score scope explicitly: team size, budget, revenue, reporting line, board exposure. Where the step
up is large, say so and identify what makes it defensible — adjacent scope, prior experience at
size, or a genuine stretch the user should be prepared to argue for.

## What alignment does not do

It tunes the résumé and the conversation. **It does not rewrite the public profile for one posting.**
The profile serves every opportunity simultaneously; optimizing it for a single role damages it for
the rest. Profile work is `salience-profile`.
