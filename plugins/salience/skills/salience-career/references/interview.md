# Interview Positioning

## Story bank

One story per competency the target role requires, built from the fact ledger.

```
COMPETENCY   Rebuilding a broken function
SITUATION    Hollis, Feb 2023. Three business units, three separate marketing teams,
             no shared measurement. Board couldn't get a straight answer on CAC.
ACTION       Consolidated to one org with shared measurement before touching strategy.
             14 tools to 6. Rebuilt attribution first, over objections that it was
             a distraction.
OUTCOME      Blended CAC down 34% in 11 months. Reporting lag 3 weeks to 2 days.
SOURCE       fact-001 (verified), fact-002 (verified)
ONE-LINER    "Rebuilt three siloed marketing orgs into one and took blended CAC down 34%."
SHORT        60-90 seconds - the default answer
LONG         3-4 minutes, with the objection handling. Only on a follow-up
```

**Three lengths, because the question determines the format.** "Give me an example" wants the
one-liner. "Tell me about a time" wants the short version. Only an explicit follow-up earns the
long one.

**Executives fail far more often by over-answering than under-answering.** Default to the short
version and let them pull.

### Categories to have covered

One story each, drawn from the ledger, regardless of what the role emphasizes:

- Leading through a genuine difficulty
- A decision made on incomplete information
- Conflict — with a peer, a board, or a CEO
- Building or rebuilding a team
- A cross-functional alignment nobody wanted
- Something that failed, and what changed afterward

Attribution stays honest in the story too. "Led the team that rebuilt" is the accurate phrasing at
this level and is not a weaker answer.

## The hard questions

### Why are you leaving / why did you leave
Brief, forward-facing, never critical of the employer. Criticism of the last place is heard as a
preview of what will be said about the next one — this holds even when the criticism is fair and
even when the interviewer invites it.

> "I've done what I was brought in to do. The next thing there is optimization, and I'm better used
> where the function needs rebuilding."

### A gap
One clause, then what came of it. The evasion is always more damaging than the fact.

### Compensation expectations
See `compensation.md`. Redirect once; give a researched range when pressed.

### Tell me about yourself
The narrative from `../../salience-positioning/references/executive-narrative.md`, in ninety seconds.
Arc, move, why now. Not a chronological walk through the résumé.

### A failure
A real one, with what changed afterward. A disguised humblebrag ("I care too much") fails this
question visibly and costs more than the failure would have.

> "I spent two quarters at Northbay optimizing creative for a funnel whose measurement was broken.
> I trusted the dashboard because it was the dashboard we'd always used. Now I audit the measurement
> layer before I accept any performance narrative, including my own."

### Level, currency, or age, asked obliquely
Answer the underlying question — currency, energy, adaptability — with evidence rather than
reassurance. Never defensive, never apologetic, and never a claim that could not be demonstrated in
the next ten minutes.

### Why should we hire you
Position, not enthusiasm. The specific match between the problem they have and the thing the user
has done before.

### The weakness question

Real weakness, genuine self-awareness, and the specific thing being done about it. A disguised
strength ("I care too much", "I hold people to a high standard") fails visibly at this level and
costs more than the honest answer would.

> "I under-communicate upward when things are going well. I treat no news as good news, and twice
> that has meant a board heard something late that they should have heard early. I now send a short
> written update on a fixed cadence whether or not there is news."

Real, specific, already being addressed, and it does not undermine the candidacy.

## Questions to ask

An executive who asks nothing sharp is not evaluating, and interviewers at this level read that as
a signal about how the person will operate.

- What is the actual mandate for the first year, as distinct from the job description?
- What has been tried already, and why did it not work?
- Who owns demand, product marketing, and brand today, and where do those lines get argued?
- How will you know at twelve months whether this worked?
- Why is this seat open, and what happened to the last person in it?
- What does success look like at 30, 60, and 90 days — and who decides?

**Do not ask:** anything answerable from the website · compensation or benefits in an early
conversation · anything phrased as yes/no · anything that reads as auditioning rather than
evaluating.
- Where does marketing sit in the operating cadence — what meetings, what reporting?
- What is the relationship between the CEO and the board on marketing spend?

The last three surface the risks that make executive roles fail. An answer that is vague on why the
seat is open is itself information.

## Mutual evaluation

The process runs in both directions from the first conversation. An executive who behaves like a
supplicant is read as the wrong level — not because confidence is a performance, but because someone
who does not evaluate the role will not push back once in it, and every board knows it.

Prepare the user to decline gracefully. Withdrawing well from a bad fit preserves a relationship with
a firm that will run other searches.
