# Positioning Frameworks

## The five questions, expanded

### 1. Who, specifically?
Push until it disqualifies someone. Stage, size, model, ownership, and the *situation*.

| Too broad | Specific enough |
|---|---|
| B2B companies | B2B SaaS, $20M-$200M ARR |
| Growing companies | Post-Series-C or PE-backed, where growth has flattened |
| Marketing leaders | CEOs who inherited a marketing function they can't evaluate |

The situation matters more than the firmographics. Two companies of identical size in different
situations are different buyers.

### 2. What problem?
In the buyer's words, as they would say it to a peer — not the practice-area name.

Two things sharpen this and are usually missing:

- **The trigger.** What made them start looking *now* rather than last year? A board meeting, a
  missed quarter, a departure, a funding round. Problems get tolerated for years; triggers create
  buyers, and content aimed at the trigger outperforms content aimed at the problem.
- **The cost of doing nothing.** If the answer is "not much", there is no engagement here regardless
  of how good the offer is.

| Practice-area framing | Buyer's framing |
|---|---|
| Demand generation optimization | We're spending more and getting less |
| MarTech rationalization | We own 14 tools and can't answer a basic question |
| Brand strategy | Nobody can explain what we do in one sentence, including us |

### 2b. Capture their actual words

Write down how the audience describes the problem **in their own language**, not in the user's. This
is the single most reusable artifact positioning produces: every hook, About line, and outreach
opener draws on it, and a system that has it stops producing content that sounds like a vendor.

Sources: what people actually said on calls, the questions that recur, the phrasing in job postings
for the role, how peers describe it in their own posts.

| The user says | The buyer says |
|---|---|
| "Attribution modeling" | "We can't tell what's working" |
| "MarTech rationalization" | "We're paying for fourteen things and can't get one answer" |
| "Demand generation strategy" | "Leads are up and revenue isn't" |

Also record **what makes them hesitate** — the objection that comes up before they commit. For an
advisory practice that is usually one of: "we tried a consultant before", "we should hire
full-time", "we don't have time to onboard someone", "this feels like it needs to be internal."
Content that answers a standing objection converts far better than content that restates the
problem.

### 3. How do you solve it?
The method, and what makes it repeatable rather than personal brilliance. A buyer hiring an
executive is buying a repeatable approach; a buyer hiring a personality is buying a risk.

### 4. Why better than the alternatives?
Name the real ones. The agency, the internal hire, the other fractional, the incumbent vendor, and
**doing nothing** — which is usually the most common competitor and the one most analyses skip.

For each: what they do well, where they fail this buyer, and why this user is the better answer for
this specific situation. If the honest answer is "for most buyers the agency is fine", that is a
positioning finding, not a failure — it means the target is narrower than assumed.

### 5. So what?
The business consequence. Not "better marketing" — what changes on the P&L, in the board meeting,
or in the CEO's week.

## Statement shapes

**Internal statement** (not copy — the thing everything else derives from):

```
For [specific who] who [specific situation], [name] is the [category] that [core benefit],
because [the reason it works], unlike [the real alternative], which [where it falls short].
```

**One-liner** (ten words or fewer, sayable out loud without embarrassment):

```
I rebuild demand engines for B2B SaaS after growth stalls.
```

**Thirty seconds** (the "what do you do" answer):

```
I run marketing for B2B SaaS companies at the point where growth flattens - usually $20M
to $200M, usually PE-backed. The pattern is almost always the same: the measurement layer
broke before the marketing did, so everyone argues about creative while the real problem
sits in attribution. I fix that first, then rebuild demand against what the data actually
says. At Hollis that took blended CAC down 34% in under a year.
```

Note it names the who, the situation, the point of view, the method, and one verified proof — and
it sounds like speech, not copy.

## Competitive mapping

Name real people and firms, not categories. A map of abstractions produces an abstract position.

| Alternative | Strong at | **They win when** | Our line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-service agency | Execution capacity, breadth | The strategy is settled and the gap is hands | Owns the number, not the deliverable |
| Full-time CMO hire | Total commitment | The problem is permanent and the budget exists | Operating in week two |
| Another fractional | Similar shape | The problem really is demand-only | Measurement, demand, and brand as one system |
| Doing nothing | Free, no risk | Growth is fine and the noise is internal | The cost of a year of flat growth |

**Fill "they win when" honestly.** Every alternative beats this user somewhere, and naming where is
what makes the position credible rather than promotional. A map where the user wins every column is
a map nobody believes, including the person who wrote it.

**The sweet spot** is a customer type where the user clearly wins, competitors cannot or will not
follow, and the tradeoff is one that buyer gladly makes.

## Tests

Run every draft through all five. See the module SKILL.md for the table. The substitution test kills
the most drafts and should — if swapping a peer's name in leaves it equally true, the statement
describes a category.

## Common failures

- **The résumé position.** "20 years of marketing leadership across B2B and B2C." A history, not a
  position.
- **The capability list.** "Brand, demand, product marketing, and analytics." A job description.
- **The unfalsifiable claim.** "I drive growth through data-driven strategy." Nobody claims the
  opposite.
- **The borrowed category.** Adopting a "category of one" claim because it sounds strong. Highest
  ceiling, hardest fall. Only with proof.
- **Positioning for optionality.** Staying vague to keep every door open. It closes all of them
  slowly.
